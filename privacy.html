CalFox Privacy Policy

Effective October 27, 2026 · Applies to the CalFox iOS app and its widgets

CalFox plans your day around your calendar, your sleep and how you feel. It runs on your iPhone. A CalFox account is optional: it turns on the AI features, which go through CalFox's server to Google. There are no analytics, no ads and no tracking. Your habits, your log and everything else you enter stay on your device and in your own iCloud. We never see them, so we never sell or share them. If you connect with other CalFox users and invite them to events, the details you choose go to them through Apple's iCloud, never through us.

Data that stays on your device

Calendars. With your permission, CalFox reads the events in Apple Calendar, including Google, Outlook and iCloud calendars added there, so it can plan around what is already booked. It never adds, changes or deletes a calendar event by itself. When you tap Open in Calendar or Invite in Calendar on one of your calendar events, Apple's own event editor opens, and only what you change there is saved, to that event's calendar. You choose which calendars it reads.

Reminders. If you turn on Apple Reminders in More, CalFox asks for permission and then reads the reminders that are not completed on the lists you choose: each one's title, due date and due time, and, only if you turn on Include reminders with no date, the ones with no date. Each one shows on your day as a to-do, placed to finish before its due time. CalFox never adds, changes, completes or deletes a reminder. Marking one done in CalFox marks it done in CalFox only. The to-dos made from your reminders are kept on this device only: they are not copied to iCloud. When you are signed in, the title of one CalFox placed on your day can go in the day check, with names taken out (see the AI below). When you mark one done in CalFox, its title goes in your log like any other to-do, and your log is in iCloud if Sync with iCloud is on. When a reminder is completed or deleted in Apple Reminders, it leaves CalFox the next time CalFox reads your reminders.

Apple Health. With your permission, CalFox reads sleep, heart rate, heart rate variability, breathing, activity, workouts, mindful minutes and similar readings from Apple Health to plan a day you can do. It never writes to Health. Health readings are used on your device and are never stored in iCloud. They are never sent to the AI or to CalFox's server. If you turn on iCloud sync, your score history can say that a score moved because of your sleep or another Health reading, but not which reading it was.

Screen Time. If you allow Screen Time, in More or when your fox offers it, you pick apps, app categories or websites in Apple's own picker: the ones that pull you away from your day and, if you like, the ones that go with a Break you are working on. Apple gives CalFox only codes for what you picked, which CalFox cannot read, so it never learns which apps they are, how long you spend in each, or what you do in them. You choose a limit, and iOS tells CalFox when what you picked passes it within a part of the day (morning, afternoon, evening or night). CalFox keeps only that: when, which part of the day, whether it was the apps that pull you away or a Break's, and the limit. Your fox then asks whether your focus was down, or whether the Break happened. Once you have agreed often enough that your focus was down, it may lower your Focus a little without asking, and still asks now and then. A Break counts as having happened only when you say it did. What Screen Time reports stays on your device: it is not stored in iCloud and is never sent to the AI. Your answers are kept like your other answers: a Break you say happened goes in your log, and how you feel goes with your other states. Turn it off in More → Screen Time, or take CalFox's access away in iOS Settings → Screen Time.

What you enter. Your habits, goals, check-in answers, completed activities, scores and facts (the notes and limits on More → Privacy → Facts) live in the app's private storage on your device and in your device backups.

Your voice. When you tap a voice button to add something, to answer your fox's question or to say something in place of a form, your iPhone turns what you say into text on the device, with Apple's on-device speech recognition. The audio is never sent anywhere and is not kept. If your iPhone cannot recognize speech on the device, CalFox does not offer voice and you type instead.

The widgets. The Up Next widget reads today's plan and today's all-day calendar events from the app's storage on your device. The fox widget reads today's plan, the kind of each item on it, each habit session's chance, today's Break windows with each Break's name and chance, your wake time and bedtime, tomorrow's first item, and your fox's name and how grown it is, from the same storage. The widgets send nothing anywhere.

iCloud sync (on from setup; you can turn it off)

Setup turns on Sync with iCloud, so deleting the app does not lose what you entered, and you can turn it off or on again in More. With it on, CalFox keeps a copy of what you enter, your scores and your facts (the notes, limits and routines, with the steps you mark done or skipped) in the private iCloud database that belongs to your Apple account, so your other devices show the same plan. Apple stores it inside your account; we cannot read it. Health readings, what Screen Time reports and your sign-in are not synced. Which CalFox account your data belongs to (its user id, how you sign in, and your first name if your account gives one) is kept with it, so your other devices know whose it is. If iCloud already holds your CalFox when you sign in during setup, CalFox uses it; if it holds CalFox data that belongs to no account, CalFox asks before it uses it; if it belongs to another account, CalFox does not use it. It never merges two sets of data.

Invites (optional)

You can connect with another CalFox user by sending them a link from More → People. The link shows your first name and a picture of both foxes, which shows how grown your fox is, to anyone who has it, in Messages and when it is opened. Nothing else is shared until they tap Connect, and each link connects one person. On iOS 17 the link goes through Messages to the people you send it to: if you send it to a group, the first person to connect is the one connected and CalFox takes the others off, but until it does, what is in the shared space can reach their iPhones, though CalFox does not show it to them. So send it to one person. The two of you then share a private space in iCloud, kept in the iCloud account of whoever sent the link and counted toward that account's storage. CalFox writes into it your first name, your fox's name and how grown your fox is (it grows as you level up; your level itself is not shared), the events you invite each other to (title, colour, note, day, time, length, time zone and where) and your answers (Going, Maybe or No, and with a Maybe the other time you suggest, if you pick one). When an invite's time is busy on your day, CalFox looks at your calendar and plan on your iPhone to offer up to three free times that day; only the time you pick is sent, never your calendar. You can also share a to-do with someone you are connected with, to do it together. CalFox then writes into the shared space the to-do's name, the day it is due by (and the time, if you gave one), how long it takes, up to six start times your iPhone offers, the ones that work on the other person's iPhone or up to three that theirs offers back, the time booked or picked, whether either of you is doing it alone, and whether each of you has ticked it and if that was during the time booked (never the time you ticked it). To find those times each iPhone looks at its own calendar and plan, on that iPhone, and only the start times are sent: never what is on your calendar or plan, and never the to-do's note or place. The to-do someone shares with you becomes one of your own to-dos, kept with your other to-dos (in your iCloud when Sync with iCloud is on); when you are signed in its name can go in the day check with names taken out, as your other to-dos' names do. On an event with several guests, each guest also sees the other guests' first names, fox names, how grown their foxes are, and answers. These details are encrypted on your iPhone before they go to iCloud, so only the two of you can read them; we cannot, and there is no server of ours. The link's first name and picture are not encrypted, since the link shows them before anyone connects. Nothing else is shared: not your Health readings, how you feel, your scores, levels or odds, your other events, habits or facts, or where you are. If an event you go to has the same name as one of your Builds, CalFox counts it for that Build on your iPhone; nothing about that is shared. Invites use iCloud even when Sync with iCloud is off. When you are signed in, the title of an event someone invited you to can go in the day check with names taken out, as your other events do; its note never goes, and its place only as "a place". Remove in More → People ends a connection: the events you share leave both your days and the shared space is deleted. Thirty days after an event ends, each of you deletes from the shared space what you wrote for it, and a link nobody opened is deleted after 30 days.

Your CalFox account (optional)

You can make a CalFox account in setup or later in More, with Sign in with Apple, with Google, or with an email address and a password, or tap Not now. An account turns on the AI features described below; everything else in CalFox works the same without one. Once you sign in, your CalFox belongs to that account: if you sign out, CalFox shows a sign-in page until you sign back in, and nothing is deleted. Your account is kept for us by Google Firebase Authentication: your email address, your name if your Apple or Google account gives one, how you sign in, and a user id that Firebase makes for you. If you sign in with Apple and choose Hide My Email, CalFox gets only Apple's relay address. With an email and password, Firebase keeps your password in a form that cannot be read back, and sends you an email to verify your address and, when you ask, to reset your password; the AI stays off until your address is verified. Your iPhone keeps your sign-in in its Keychain, on this device only; it is not stored in iCloud. What you enter in CalFox is not part of your account: it stays on your device and in your own iCloud, never on a server of ours.

CalFox's server keeps a count of how many times your account asked the AI each day, by your user id, so each person stays within a daily allowance. It does not keep what an ask said.

Data that leaves your device: the AI (optional, with an account)

CalFox can use Google's Gemini to sort your activities and calendar events by kind, find habits you could do together, learn how long your activities take, word a step up and your fox's lines, suggest what leads to a Break, check a day it has planned and the days it picked for your habits in the coming week, read what you add by typing or talking, read what you say in place of filling in a form, pick out facts from your answers to your fox's questions, check a new fact against the ones you already have, and read a goal and word its milestones. This only happens when you are signed in to a CalFox account with a verified email address (Apple and Google accounts count as verified). Everything in CalFox works without it.

How it goes. Your iPhone sends each ask to CalFox's server with a sign-in token that says which account is asking, and the server sends it on to Google's Gemini API with CalFox's own key. CalFox uses Gemini's free tier: on the free tier, Google may use what it is sent to improve its products, and people at Google may read it. Health readings are never sent.

What is taken out first. Before anything goes, your iPhone takes out what could tell who you or anyone else is: every name it can spot, your own included, goes as a placeholder (Name1, Name2) that your iPhone puts back in the answer; emails, phone numbers, links, street addresses and birth dates are replaced with words such as "an email" or "a place"; a calendar event's place goes only as "a place" and its people only as placeholders; Names spotted by the phone are found by your iPhone's own language tools, so an unusual name can be missed; More → Privacy → Recent shows the text of each ask as it went, so you can check.

What can be sent. Since October 19, 2026 CalFox does not hold back whole topics: it may send what helps each ask, including how you feel, a condition such as being sick or sore, your Breaks, goals about your body, eating, health or money, why a goal matters to you and where you are now on a goal (a number you set). Since October 21, 2026 this includes the facts on More → Privacy → Facts, except those read from Apple Health, so Gemini can read their words; like everything else, they go only after your iPhone has taken out names and the other details above. When you are signed in, CalFox sends short text from your iPhone, through CalFox's server, to Google:

  • the names of your activities and the titles of calendar events (only those the phone cannot sort by itself), to tell what kind of thing each is and how long it usually takes, and never one on your Not asked about list;
  • when a habit is ready to step up, its name, its length, how often you do it, the name of the goal it builds to and how many of that goal's milestones are done;
  • when CalFox looks for two habits you could do at the same time, the names of your habits;
  • when your fox words its lines (why something is where it is on your day, a fact or experiment card, an insight, the line on a tick, including those about a Break or a goal; never one that rests on a Health reading), at most once a day and twenty lines: the kind of each line, your iPhone's own sentence with every name replaced by a placeholder, and its numbers. Your iPhone checks every line that comes back (the same placeholders and numbers, no other number, plain words) and uses its own if any check fails. The lock screen and the widgets show your iPhone's own lines;
  • when you say something instead of filling in a form (your work or school hours and other routines, how a usual morning or evening goes when your fox asks, how an activity goes for you, a normal weekday, an answer to a goal's question, a step up of your own, a new habit): what you said (as text, never audio), once, and the form's own choices, to read what your iPhone could not. Your words are not kept; More → Privacy shows the ask with them left out. You see everything it read in the form and confirm it before anything is saved;
  • when you pick what leads to a Break, the Break's name, to suggest the states most likely to lead to a slip;
  • when you add something by typing or talking, the words you typed or said (as text, never audio), the date, the time now and your time zone, and the names of your habits, so CalFox can tell whether it is something you did, an event, a to-do or a habit and fill in its day and time. You see and can change everything it read before anything is added;
  • when you answer one of your fox's open questions by typing or talking: the question, what you said (as text, never audio) and the names of your habits, once, so CalFox can offer you a few short facts to keep. Your answer itself is not kept; only the facts you tick are kept, on your Facts page;
  • when you add a goal that CalFox cannot read by itself, its name and the names of the habits linked to it, to tell what kind of goal it is, its unit, and a number and a date the name states. A goal about your body or money keeps its kind and gets no number from Gemini;
  • when CalFox plans milestones for a goal with no number (such as learning a language, or a goal about your body or money), the goal's name and kind, how many milestones and the weeks to its date, the days a week you can give it, where you are now (a number you set; never one from Health), why it matters to you and what gets in the way. Your iPhone checks every milestone that comes back (in order, in the future, at a safe pace, in plain words, and for a goal about your body or money something to do, never a weight, a size, a calorie or an amount) and uses its own list if any of them fails;
  • when you add a goal or a habit and answer its follow-up questions, what you said, the questions' topics, and today's date, to read your answers and word the next short question; your iPhone checks every question and uses its own if any check fails. For a goal about your body or money it never asks for a number, keeps no number Gemini reads, and drops any question with a weight or an amount in it;
  • when an event or something you log matches none of your habits by name, its title with names taken out, and the names of your habits with whether each is a Build or a Break and its kind, to tell if it counts for one of them; the answer must be one of those names or none, and it counts only after you say yes;
  • when you add a fact, that fact and your other facts about the same thing, to find two that cannot both be true; your iPhone checks that both are still kept, and nothing changes until you pick which one is right;
  • when CalFox checks the days and times it picked for your habits in the coming week, at most once a day: each habit's name and length, the day and time picked, the busy times of each day without their titles, and the facts described in the next item. Gemini can only say which pick looks wrong and why; your iPhone's own rules set every time. Nothing moves;
  • when CalFox checks a day it has planned, at most once a day: what it planned for you (habits, meals, breaks, suggestions and the to-dos it placed, including those from Reminders) with their times; your calendar events with their kinds, titles and times, whether each has a place or people (as "a place" and placeholders), and for an event on your Not asked about list only its time; your facts on More → Privacy → Facts, except those read from Apple Health, as many as fit in a short list; and for today, how you feel and a condition you are in (such as sore, day 2). Gemini can only say which item looks wrong and why; the note shows under that item, and your iPhone's own rules set every time. Nothing moves.

Never sent: audio, Health readings, your password, a calendar event's notes, anything from Screen Time, or anything when you are signed out. Health readings and what Screen Time reports are never stored in iCloud. Google handles this text under Google's terms for CalFox's use of the Gemini API. Google requires Gemini API users to be 18 or older, and the AI features are for people 18 or older. More → Privacy lists what was sent lately under Recent, with the text of each ask as it went.

Places: Apple Maps

When an event you add names a place, such as "Trivia at Brewster's pub", CalFox looks up that place's name in Apple Maps to fill in where the event is. Only the place's name is sent, to Apple. Your location, a person's name and the rest of what you said are never sent, and CalFox does not ask for your location. This happens whether or not you are signed in. You see the place it found and can clear it before anything is added.

Notifications

Reminders for your Runup, your Rundown and your habits are scheduled on your device. They are optional and can be turned off in More or in iOS Settings. With iCloud sync on, Apple may send silent notifications to tell the app that new data is waiting in your iCloud.

When someone invites you, moves or cancels an event, CalFox shows a notification with the title, day and time, never the place or the note, and you can answer Accept, Maybe or Decline from it. When someone shares a to-do with you, or a time is booked for one you share, the notification says the to-do's name and when it is due or booked, with nothing to answer. Your answer goes to them through iCloud, as it does from the app. iCloud may send silent notifications to tell CalFox an invite changed. CalFox writes the notification itself on your iPhone, so the time is in your time zone. These silent notifications can come late, and they do not reach CalFox after you close it from the app switcher; the invite then shows the next time you open CalFox. Notifications for invites are asked for once, the first time you use invites, and can be turned off in iOS Settings; your fox still brings invites in the app.

TestFlight

If you test CalFox through TestFlight, Apple shares crash reports and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send with the developer, under Apple's TestFlight terms.

Deleting your data

Your account: More → Delete account deletes your CalFox account from Firebase and the daily count CalFox's server keeps for it, and everything you entered: your habits, goals, scores, history and facts, on this device and, with iCloud sync on, in your iCloud and on your other devices. It also removes you from the people you connected with. If you signed in with Apple, CalFox also asks Apple to revoke CalFox's sign-in. Setup then starts over. Sign out in More signs you out on that device and deletes nothing.

In the app: More → Erase all my data deletes your habits, goals, scores, history and facts, and what Screen Time reported. With iCloud sync on, it deletes them from your other devices and your iCloud too. Erase all my data also removes you from the people you connected with and deletes the shared spaces kept in your iCloud. Turn off calendar, Reminders, Health and Screen Time access in iOS Settings.

On your iPhone: deleting the app deletes everything it stored on the device. To delete an iCloud copy, go to iOS Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage → CalFox. If you delete the app first, a shared space stays in iCloud until you remove it there.

We hold no copy of your data. On our side there is only your account and its daily count, which Delete account removes.

Children

CalFox is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. The optional AI features are for people 18 or older, under Google's terms.

Permissions the app asks for

Calendars (full access): plan around your events; an event changes only when you edit it yourself in Apple's event editor. Apple Health (read only): plan around your sleep and how you feel. Reminders (full access, read only): place the reminders on the lists you choose on your day as to-dos; CalFox never changes a reminder. Microphone and Speech Recognition: only while you tap a voice button to add something, answer your fox's question or say something in place of a form; the words are worked out on your iPhone and the audio is never sent. Screen Time (only if you allow it): be told when the apps you pick pass your limit in a part of the day; CalFox never sees which apps they are or what you do in them. Notifications: optional reminders and invites. Each can be declined or turned off in iOS Settings. CalFox still works without them, with less to plan from.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date.
